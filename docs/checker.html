Psychometric Interpretation Guide
Hide Guidelines ▲
Difficulty Index (P) [Ebel, 1979]
Definition: Proportion of students answering correctly (0% to 100%).
  • 50%–85% (Optimal Zone): Maximizes measurement accuracy and assessment spread [Ebel, 1979].
  • >85% (Easy): Prerequisite/confidence check; low differentiation.
  • 20%–49% (Hard): High rigor; effectively separates top achievers.
  • <20% (Very Hard): Flagged. Check for confusing wording, unaddressed content, or wrong key [Ebel, 1979].
Discrimination Index (r_pbis) [Ebel, 1979]
Definition: Point-biserial correlation between item score and total score.
  • ≥0.30 (Strong): Excellent item. High-scorers get it right; low-scorers miss it [Ebel, 1979].
  • 0.20–0.29 (Acceptable): Standard discriminating power.
  • 0.00–0.19 (Weak): Fails to clearly separate mastery levels.
  • <0.00 (Critical!): Negative discrimination. Low-scorers scored higher than high-scorers. Check answer key first! [Ebel, 1979].
Distractor Efficiency [Haladyna, 1993]
Definition: How students distribute across incorrect options.
  • Dead Distractor (0%): No student picked it. Replace with plausible distractor [Haladyna & Downing, 1993].
  • Functional Distractor: Attracts low-scorers while high-scorers avoid it.
  • Attractive Misconception: If chosen heavily by cohort (>25%), indicates a misleading stem or widespread misconception.
Reliability (α / KR-20) [Cronbach, 1951]
Definition: Internal consistency reliability of the exam (0.0 to 1.0) [Cronbach, 1951; Kuder & Richardson, 1937].
  • ≥0.80 (High): Reliable for critical summative decisions [Cronbach, 1951].
  • 0.70–0.79 (Good): Standard benchmark for classroom unit exams.
  • <0.70 (Moderate): Increase item count or revise low/negative discrimination items.
